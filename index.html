    Ocean Zones

    The deeper you go into the ocean, the environment changes. That's why the ocean is divided into 5 major zones, each having their own characteristics. The ocean is very deep and there are a lot of parts that are yet undiscovered...

    The Sunlight Zone

    this zone stretches from 0 meters to 200 meters deep (0-656ft). This zone recieves the most sunlight out of any other zone and that is the reason why majority of sea creatures live in this section of the ocean. Sunlight allows for photosynthesis to happen which causes plants to grow and in the long term causes a chain of food source. plankton eat the plants, small fishes eat the plankton and other animals eat the small fishes.

    The Twilight Zone

    There is a less abundance of animals in the twilight zone because here, the sunlight fades dramatically. No plants can survive here! The twilight zone stretches from 200-1000 meters(656-3280ft). This is where animals with bioluminescent features on their body start to make an appearance. The ocean gets colder, while the pressure starts to stack up.

    The Midnight Zone

    The midnight zone stretches from 1000-400 meters (3280-13,123ft) below the ocean. Up until this point No Sunlight reaches This zone is even darker, colder, and worse pressure making it harsher than the twilight zone. One of the most common animals to inhabit this area is the angler fish.

    The Abysall zone

    The abysall zone stretches from 4000-6000 meters (13,123-19685)below the ocean. This zone is even harsher than the midnight zone. Waters almost reaching freezing temperatures, pressure being unimaginable, and very dark. very few animals live in this zone since food is scarce too.

    The Hadal Zone

    This is the last and of course the harshest zone in the entire ocean. From 6000 meters to the very bottom, the hadal zone is the coldest part of the ocean. These depths can only occur in trenches. It's pitch black, near freezing, with pressure that is much worse than the abyssal zone. Only a few select of sea creatures are capable enough to survive in this zone.

    Why is the Ocean important

    The ocean is important for our survival and because of it, we humans can thrive on earth. Oceans produce more than half of the earth's oxygen while also absorbing carbon dioxide 50 times more effectively than the atmosphere itself. Overall the ocean helps maintain our air quality as good as possible. Another way oceans help us is by regulating the climate. As the sun hits the earth, oceans absorb the heat, which then gets transfered into the atmosphere. After that, the atmosphere spreads heat throughout the world. Oceans also release heat at a slow pace and this prevents extreme hot and cold temperatures to occur. On top of this, oceans also provide tons of food and job opportunities for humans.

    Threats of the ocean

    While oceans play a huge role in making the earth a livable enviroment for all living organisms there are a lot of factors that are damaging it right now!

    Pollution: over 8 million metric tons of just plastic go into oceans each year. This obviously harms the enviroment as plastic takes forerver to decompose. As time goes by, plastic slowly invades the ocean while also disrupting habitats, and making it unsanitary.

    Drilling & Mining: One of the most valuable resources that humans extract from the ocean is oil and the process to extract this resource is called offshore drilling. A machine using a substance called drilling fluid drills in the ocean floor and then take the oil out from the area. Other resources are also extracted from oceans by mining which include minerals such as copper, lead, silver, gold, nickel, and sea salt just toname a few. These processes are harmful to the environment because not only do they disrupt ocean habitats, metals andtoxic chemicals can be released into the oceans, causing contamination.
